# SCA Standartları Tarif Değil Yöntem Rehberidir

As of **16 October 2026**, the Specialty Coffee Association (SCA) publishes no universal, method-specific recipe standard covering all eight brewing methods in Brew App. Its current catalogue identifies **SCA-310 for home coffee-brewer specifications and test methods** and **SCA-350 for semi-automatic and automatic espresso-machine specifications and test methods**; these are equipment standards, not endorsements of an app's recipe. The catalogue separately lists coffee-evaluation standards and shows other equipment standards still in development. Therefore, Brew App can accurately give practical, adjustable recipes, but should reserve “SCA-compliant” or “SCA-certified” claims for the scope actually tested or certified by SCA. ([SCA standards catalogue](https://sca.coffee/research/coffee-standards); [SCA Certified Home Equipment](https://sca.coffee/certified-home-brewer))

## Current SCA standards address equipment, not these eight recipes

The SCA’s published standards catalogue distinguishes coffee assessment from equipment testing. SCA-102 covers sample preparation and tasting mechanics, while SCA-103 and SCA-104 cover descriptive and affective assessment; none is a consumer brewing recipe. For equipment, SCA-310 is titled “Home Coffee Brewers: Specifications and Test Methods,” and SCA-350 is “Semi-Automatic and Automatic Espresso Machines: Specifications and Test Methods.” The SCA’s home-equipment page describes certification as third-party testing of particular machines for brewing quality, consistency, and performance. That product-level certification does not make a V60, a recipe, or an app SCA-certified. ([SCA standards catalogue](https://sca.coffee/research/coffee-standards); [SCA Certified Home Equipment](https://sca.coffee/certified-home-brewer))

The current catalogue does **not** list a general SCA brewing-recipe standard or a standalone “Golden Cup” method standard. Familiar historical SCA brewing-chart numbers should therefore not be presented as current mandatory SCA requirements for every brew style. The same catalogue labels **SCA-315, Café Batch Coffee Brewers**, and **SCA-352, Fully Automatic Espresso Machines**, as under development; they are not published standards to claim compliance with. ([SCA standards catalogue](https://sca.coffee/research/coffee-standards))

## Only espresso and machine brewers have adjacent equipment standards

| Brew App method | Relevant current SCA standard | Accurate in-app framing |
|---|---|---|
| Espresso | SCA-350 covers semi-automatic and automatic espresso-machine specifications and tests. | It concerns equipment, not a universal espresso dose, yield, pressure, or shot-time recipe. |
| V60 / pour-over | No method-specific recipe standard listed. SCA-310 is for home-brewer equipment, not a manual V60 recipe. | Offer an adjustable pour-over guide; do not call it SCA-compliant. |
| Chemex | No Chemex-specific standard listed. | Present the recipe as app guidance, with filter and brewer-specific adjustments. |
| AeroPress | No AeroPress-specific standard listed. | Label recipe parameters as suggested starting points, not SCA requirements. |
| French press | No French-press-specific standard listed. | Make steeping, grind, and filtration choices adjustable; do not imply certification. |
| Moka pot | No moka-pot-specific standard listed. | Treat stovetop instructions as method guidance, not SCA standardization. |
| Turkish coffee | No Turkish-coffee-specific standard listed. | Provide culturally and equipment-aware instructions without an SCA compliance label. |
| Cold brew | No cold-brew method standard listed. SCA-315 is a café batch-brewer equipment standard still in development—not a published cold-brew recipe. | Keep cold-water steeping guidance separate from hot-brew and equipment-standard claims. |

These distinctions follow the SCA’s published-standard and in-development lists; they do not mean SCA has judged any method or recipe inferior. The standards define narrower scopes than “coffee brewing” in everyday usage. ([SCA standards catalogue](https://sca.coffee/research/coffee-standards))

## Keep the guidance useful without implying certification

Brew App should name its recipes **“Brew App starting recipes”** or **“suggested parameters,”** and identify their source and revision date. For each method, show the dose and water amount, grind guidance, water temperature when applicable, contact or pour schedule, and expected beverage yield; let people adjust these to their coffee, equipment, and taste. Make clear where a recipe is a house recommendation or is adapted from a named third-party source. Avoid “SCA recipe,” “SCA approved,” “SCA compliant,” and SCA marks unless the specific claim and product have documented authorization.

For espresso machines and certified home-brewer equipment, explain the limited relationship precisely: SCA-350 and SCA-310 test **specified equipment**; only named models with current SCA certification should be described as SCA-certified, and only in the certified category. A method guide used on that equipment does not inherit its certification. The catalogue says the certification-standard texts are member-access materials, so link the public catalogue and avoid asserting detailed pass thresholds unless Brew App has verified them from an authorized copy. ([SCA standards catalogue](https://sca.coffee/research/coffee-standards); [SCA Certified Home Equipment](https://sca.coffee/certified-home-brewer))

## Conclusion

The safe product distinction is straightforward: **SCA publishes relevant equipment standards, not a current universal recipe rulebook for these eight methods.** Brew App can still deliver actionable, method-specific advice by making its recipe provenance and adjustable nature explicit—and by keeping equipment certification claims attached only to the particular certified machines and test scope.
